# The Hanzi Project

## Background and Inspiration:

For background, I am a total nerd about language learning. I have learned Japanese to a JLPT N1 level and Taiwanese Mandarin to
quite an advanced level, too.

While I was learning Japanese in Japan, I remember I found this useful website—I unfortunately forgot the name—
where you are shown a kanji, and you must simply decide whether you know it or not. If you say yes, you get a green dot
that appears somewhere on a line graph, or if you say no, then a red dot appears somewhere. Where it is placed on the graph
and how many points it takes away from you (how many kanji it predicts you know based on your answers) is determined based on
the difficulty of the characters that you've answered, that is to say how frequently it shows up in Japanese media and daily life. I have searched and searched for such a thing in Mandarin, let alone Taiwanese Mandarin, and have come up short. I hope this can be useful for people like me.

The design was quite crude, and there was no great penalty or gain from saying whether you know a character or not, and no
real way to test you, either. You just have to be honest enough with yourself for an accurate score. The point is to
learn how many kanji you recognize.

The reason I fell in love with Chinese and Japanese to begin with is because of the script. It mesmerizes me like nothing else on Earth,
and I notice as I learn Chinese these days, I am more concerned about whether I recognize a character and its reading
over its usefulness in the real world. I get quite excited when I encounter a character I've never seen before, to the point I
make an effort to never encounter unknown characters anymore.

While my obsession clearly is niche, I know there must be some people in the world—foreign or native—speakers of those languages in the world
that are curious about how many characters they can recognize.

## Ideas for the Future:

I want to implement things in my program that are vastly more engaging, useful, and accurate than that Japanese website.
I want to, as a base, take from a similar database of random characters at a time and quiz the user, but not just arbitrarily
decide whether they *think* they know the character or not. No, I want to quiz them to see if they know the pronunciation.
So, I will ask the user if they know the pinyin spelling, and eventually, when I progress the program enough, ask them if they know
the zhuyin or not. Maybe I'll let them choose.

Anyway, other than this, I want to make it not just about Taiwanese Mandarin and traditional characters—although I do
see there is a cruel bias for mainland and simplified Chinese in tools and resources as opposed to Taiwan, and since I have
a Taiwanese partner and currently live in Taiwan, I want to prioritize being Taiwan's representation. I also wish to use
the program for myself as I progress in the language and learn more and more characters.

In addition to Taiwanese traditional mode and mainland simplified mode, I also want to expand to include a Cantonese version,
and also a Japanese version, since they all use similar characters.

I have many more exciting ideas for the future, so stay tuned!
